Family story

Cheryl remembers that this recipe evolved from a GQ article Michael passed along to Dan years ago. The original article has not yet been located, so the family version is preserved here as Cheryl submitted it.

At Afterglow, German Pancake became something everyone expected, with Cabin 5 coming over when it was ready — Dan’s whistle was the signal. Back at “1103,” it remains a staple whenever Ellie and Josie visit, each with her own favorite version.

Method

About the handwritten-style family instructions
  • The base uses precisely the quantities Cheryl provided: 2 cups flour, 2 tablespoons sugar, ¼ teaspoon salt, 2 eggs, ½ cup milk, and 2–3 tablespoons butter. These proportions have not been adjusted or “corrected.”
  • Ellie adds a small handful of chocolate chips to the batter before baking.
  • Josie switches to cast iron with sautéed cinnamon apples, changes the milk to ⅔ cup, and bakes at 425°F for 23 minutes before finishing with cinnamon sugar.
  • Cheryl did not specify the number of apples, their sauté time, or a serving yield. These details are intentionally left open rather than invented.
Original GQ article found — December 2010

Cheryl found the original magazine clipping! It is Adam Rapoport’s The Technique > The High-Rise Pancake, featuring a recipe called The Big Pancake, from GQ, December 2010, page 156 (the photograph credit is partly obscured in the clipping).

The magazine version specifies ½ cup flour, rather than the 2 cups in Cheryl’s submitted family recipe. It also uses 1 teaspoon sugar rather than 2 tablespoons, bakes at 425°F for 18–20 minutes rather than 400°F for 22 minutes, and includes optional nutmeg. The eggs, milk, salt, butter, and lemon-and-powdered-sugar finish are similar. The family instructions and Ellie’s and Josie’s versions remain exactly as submitted.
