Overnight White Bread

Source: Ken Forkish, Flour Water Salt Yeast, “Overnight White Bread”
Makes: 2 loaves
Family story

Michael started making artisan bread early in the pandemic, when the extra time at home made slow doughs and overnight fermentation a natural kitchen project. This one became a favorite partly because of how perfectly it lives up to the name of Ken Forkish's cookbook: Flour Water Salt Yeast.

Those four ingredients really are the whole loaf — flour, water, salt, and yeast — with time doing most of the rest. There is something especially satisfying about turning such a short ingredient list into a deeply browned, crackling loaf, and this is one Michael has kept coming back to.

Method

Family note — Michael's one-loaf version
  • The handwritten notes in the scan halve the cookbook formula to one loaf: 500 grams white flour, 390 grams water, 11 grams fine sea salt, and 0.4 gram instant dried yeast.
  • The simple schedule written there is: mix around 7 p.m., shape around 8 a.m., bake around 9:15 a.m., and out of the oven around 10 a.m.
  • Choose ½× above to match those handwritten one-loaf quantities.
From the source
  • The cookbook describes this as a crusty white bread with an open interior and notes that the long overnight bulk fermentation develops more flavor.
  • The source says the recipe is also suitable for focaccia or iron-skillet pizza.
  • Hot Dutch oven: use oven mitts and take care to keep hands and forearms away from the preheated pot.
